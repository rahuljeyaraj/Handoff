# Schematic review before layout — 11 Sep 2026

Reviewed: `handoff.kicad_sch` as generated by `tools/gen_schematic.py` at
commit f7736f4, against `README.md` and design §5–§7, §12–§13, §15. Every
check below was done on the regenerated sheet; the generator ends
`ERC: 0 violation(s), 0 error(s)`, `netlist: 54 nets, 0 missing, 0 unexpected`,
`README table diff: 0 difference(s)`.

**Verdict in one line:** the wiring was right; the circuit has one way to be
dead on arrival (GP2 leakage into a 10 MΩ node, §2.4) and one way to miss its
own timing budget (recovery after a shout, §2.3). Both are now measurable and
fixable on the bench without a rework, and the first one is preventable in
firmware. Nothing here requires a part the owner has not bought.

Headline findings, in order of consequence:

1. **GP2 leakage × R3 (10 MΩ) sets stage 1's DC point.** 15 nA of leakage
   uses up stage 1's whole headroom; RP2350-E9 can source 46 nA through this
   path and park OUT1 on the rail. Firmware must run GP2 with its input buffer
   disabled when high-Z; C6 (DNP) is the hardware escape. §2.4.
2. **Recovery after the receiver's own shout is 3–4 ms on a body, not 1 ms**,
   set by R3 × C_pad; and up to 13 ms more if the OOK-off chips drive GP2 low.
   Firmware: off-chips high-Z, never idle GP2 driven. Hardware: R3 = 1 MΩ
   brings it to 0.4 ms for 0.6 dB at 200 kHz — recommended, owner's call. §2.3.
3. **Mains hum will clip stage 2 long before the carrier does**: 13 mV at the
   10 MΩ node is the ×121 limit and the interstage high-pass is at 16 Hz.
   C1 = 330 pF (a part on hand) moves it to 4.8 kHz and buys 40 dB at 50 Hz.
   Recommended, owner's call; JP3 exists for the day it is not enough. §2.2.
4. The "42-pad" Pico footprint is a grep artefact: 40 pads. §1.3.
5. J4 was never in the README parts table; the new BOM check caught it.

## 2. Will it work from every direction

### 2.1 DC operating point — **OK, with one firmware condition**

- VREF = 3.3 × 100 k / 200 k = 1.65 V; divider draws 16.5 µA; C4 gives it a
  0.3 Hz corner, so VREF is quiet.
- U2A +IN via R3: I_B = 1 pA typ (60 pA at 85 °C) × 10 MΩ = 0.6 mV. OUT A =
  VREF. C1/R6 re-bias IN B to VREF; OUT B = VREF. ADC0 idles at 1.65 V = 2048
  LSB. No stage returns to a rail through a wrong reference.
- The **only** other DC path into the 10 MΩ node is GP2 → R1 → R2 → R3 → VREF,
  a 2 MΩ : 10 MΩ divider: HIZ = VREF + (V_GP2 − 1.65) × 10/12. GP2 driven low
  → HIZ = 0.27 V → OUT A on the low rail; driven high → 3.02 V → high rail.
  **Firmware must never leave GP2 as a driven output between shouts** — design
  §6.3's high-Z rule is not a nicety, it is the bias network.

### 2.2 AC — **gain and corners as designed; clipping is the risk, and it is hum, not carrier**

- MCP6292 GBW 10 MHz / noise gain 11 = 909 kHz per stage; two cascaded →
  585 kHz. Fine at 200 kHz.
- R6/C1 high-pass 15.9 Hz. R9/C2 low-pass 322 kHz (−1.6 dB at 200 kHz).
- Pad → HIZ transfer is set by R2 against the 10 pF input capacitance, not by
  R3: −22 dB at 200 kHz, −9 dB at 40 kHz, 0 dB at 50 Hz. (R3 = 1 MΩ instead
  of 10 MΩ costs 0.6 dB at 200 kHz, 2.4 dB at 40 kHz, 3 dB at 50 Hz — see 2.3.)
- Link budget: 1.3 mV at HIZ × 121 = 157 mV at the ADC, ~195 LSB. ✓
- **Clipping:** rail-to-rail output, ±1.62 V about VREF. Stage 2 clips at
  **13 mV peak at HIZ** (27 mV p-p) — 20 dB above the wanted signal. Stage 1
  alone (JP3 to OUT1) clips at 147 mV peak.
- **Interferer:** the pad has no high-pass at all and the interstage one is at
  16 Hz, so mains hum passes at full gain. A body carrying 1 V of 50 Hz (an
  ordinary figure indoors) through ~100 pF of skin–pad capacitance into the
  11 MΩ pad node puts 0.23 V on HIZ: **stage 1 clips**, never mind stage 2.
  This is design §15.2's risk with numbers. Mitigations, cheapest first:
  1. **C1 = 330 pF** (the C2 part, same 1206 pad) with R6 = 100 k → high-pass at
     4.8 kHz: −40 dB at 50 Hz, −0.06 dB at 40 kHz. Stage 2's hum limit rises
     11× to 147 mV at HIZ, and the interstage time constant falls from 10 ms
     to 33 µs (see 2.3). Recommended; not applied — it changes a design-doc
     value and is a one-part swap at M7. Note the residual: stage 1 still sees
     the hum, so the limit becomes stage 1's 147 mV.
  2. **JP3 to OUT1** (×11): the hall-of-projects fallback, 20 dB more headroom.
  3. The §15.2 LC bandpass: see §3, not added.
- Noise, rough: the 1 MΩ source shunted to ~80 kΩ by C_in at 200 kHz gives
  36 nV/√Hz; with the op-amp's 8.7 nV/√Hz, 3.7 µV rms in a 10 kHz Goertzel
  bin at HIZ against 1.3 mV signal: **51 dB SNR** before processing gain. Not
  the limit.

### 2.3 The shout and the recovery — **1 ms is not met as drawn on a body; firmware can halve the damage, R3 = 1 MΩ fixes it**

- Contrary to the brief's premise, on a body the receiver is *not* saturated
  at AC by its own carrier: the pad node swing is 3.3 V × 5.3 kΩ / 1 MΩ ≈
  18 mV, HIZ 1.3 mV, ADC 157 mV — the same level the far end hears. On the
  bench with no body (C_pad ≈ 10 pF) the pad swings 240 mV, HIZ 18 mV, ×121 =
  2.2 V p-p: stage 2 clips. So M8's "capacitor as fake body" is the realistic
  case; M5/M7 without it will show clipping that is an artefact.
- What saturates the receiver during its own shout is **DC**: the OOK-off chips.
  If an off-chip drives GP2 low, HIZ heads for 0.27 V with τ = 2 MΩ × C_pad ≈
  0.3 ms on a body — most of the way within a 500 µs chip. Stage 1 clips on
  every off-chip. If off-chips are high-Z instead, HIZ drifts back toward VREF
  with τ = 1.6 ms and never gets far. **Firmware: OOK-off = high-Z (input
  buffer off), and end every shout in high-Z.**
- **Recovery, stage 1:** HIZ returns to VREF through R3 with C_pad + C_in.
  R3 = 10 MΩ, 160 pF (on a body): τ = 1.6 ms, 3.6 ms to come off the rail
  (from 1.375 V to 145 mV). Bench, 20 pF: 0.45 ms. **On a body the design's
  1 ms (§9.7) is missed by 3.6×.** Same numbers with R3 = 1 MΩ: 0.36 ms on a
  body, 40 µs on the bench — inside budget with margin.
- **Recovery, stage 2:** C1/R6 τ = 10 ms. If OUT1 spends the shout's off-chips
  on the low rail, its mean sits 0.83 V below VREF and C1 acquires 0.52 V in a
  10 ms shout; stage 2 then stays clipped for a further **13 ms**. With
  off-chips high-Z the mean stays at VREF and C1 acquires nothing. With C1 =
  330 pF (2.2 above) τ = 33 µs and this term vanishes whatever firmware does.
- **Recommendation:** R3 = 1 MΩ (the R1/R2 part, same pad) and C1 = 330 pF. Cost:
  0.6 dB at 200 kHz, 2.4 dB at 40 kHz; benefit: 10× faster recovery, 10× less
  leakage sensitivity, 40 dB of hum rejection at stage 2. Both are value
  changes on existing 1206 pads, so the schematic is untouched and M7/M8 can
  A/B them. Not applied: design-doc values, owner's decision. M8's turnaround
  measurement is the test; M13 runs 10 000 of them.

### 2.4 GP2 high-Z leakage (RP2350-E9) — **problem; mandatory firmware measure, hardware escape provided**

- With GP2 high-Z every nA that GP2 sources or sinks flows through R3 (the
  pad is capacitive, C_pad is open at DC): HIZ shifts by I × 10 MΩ. Stage 1's
  headroom is ±147 mV → **leakage must be under 15 nA** (145 nA with R3 = 1 MΩ).
- E9 (A2-stepping RP2350, confirmed against the erratum: GPIO as input, input
  buffer enabled, pad between logic levels → up to 120 µA, the pad self-latching
  near 2.1–2.2 V). Our pad node idles at VREF = 1.65 V, inside that window. If
  GP2 latches at 2.2 V: 46 nA through R1 + R2 + R3, HIZ +0.46 V, OUT1 +5 V →
  **stage 1 pinned to the rail, receiver dead.** With R3 = 1 MΩ it is still
  +2 V on OUT1: R3 does not save it. The A4 stepping (July 2025) fixes E9;
  which stepping the owner's Pico 2 W carries is **not verified** — read
  `rp2350_chip_version()` at M3.
- Firmware fix, documented by Raspberry Pi: disable the input buffer
  (`gpio_set_input_enabled(2, false)`) whenever GP2 is high-Z. Then the pin's
  leakage is the ordinary junction figure, typically nA, but the datasheet
  limit is ±1 µA — so **measure it**: with JP2 bridged and GP2 high-Z, TP2
  reads 1.65 V if clean and a rail if not. That is the M3 exit criterion
  "GP2 reads as high-Z" made concrete.
- Hardware escape, added: **C6 (DNP, 330 pF C0G, the C2 part) in parallel with
  JP2.** Leave JP2 open and fit C6: the transmitter is AC-coupled, no DC from
  GP2 can reach the pad node whatever the stepping or the firmware does.
  Cost to the carrier: 2.4 kΩ at 200 kHz, 12 kΩ at 40 kHz, against R1's 1 MΩ
  — nil. It does not change the recovery numbers in 2.3 (those are the pad
  node's own return through R3), and the chip-rate envelope still saturates
  the receiver during its own shout, as accepted.
- JP2 → "route TX from a J4 pin" does not help with E9 (every bank-0 pin has
  it); the README no longer suggests it as a fix.

### 2.5 Power — **OK**

- 1N5819: V_F ≈ 0.2 V at 30 mA (Pico idle), 0.35 V at 200 mA (radio bursts).
  At the 3.0 V cell cut-off VSYS = 2.65 V; the RT6154 needs 1.8 V. ✓
- USB in, SW1 off: VBUS → Pico's own MBR120 → VSYS ≈ 4.7 V; D1 sees 4.7 V
  reverse at most (40 V part), anode floating. Nothing reaches the cell. ✓
- USB in, SW1 on: VSYS ≈ 4.7 V from VBUS vs 4.2 − 0.35 = 3.85 V from a full
  cell; D1 is reverse-biased, the cell neither charges nor discharges.
  **Acceptable — that is what D1 is for.** Worst case (VBUS sagging to 4.4 V,
  cell at 4.2 V) USB still wins by 0.2 V.
- Reversed J1: D1 sees 3.7–4.2 V reverse; nothing powers; TP10 reads negative.
  ✓ Harmless.
- Both cell plugs (J1, J5) are before SW1: J5 cannot power the board and the
  charger cannot be back-driven by USB. A TP4056 module with its OUT± on J5
  and its own USB plugged in while the board's USB is also in: two mains-
  referenced grounds meet only through the cell's negative — acceptable on
  the bench, **forbidden while anyone touches an electrode (design §13)**.
- The cell: whether the KP384455 pack carries its own protection PCB is
  **not verified**. If it does not, nothing on this board limits a short
  (no fuse, no polyfuse). Confirm before M11.

### 2.6 VSYS/3 monitor — **note for firmware, recorded in the README**

GP29 reads VSYS = V_cell − V_F(D1): add 0.2–0.35 V depending on load, or
calibrate once against TP10. On a Pico 2 W the reading needs the CYW43 CS
dance (`pico-examples/adc/read_vsys`).

### 2.7 LED currents — **not a problem for a status LED; blue may be dim**

3.3 V − 0.1 V GPIO drop − V_F, through 330 Ω: red (2.0 V) 3.6 mA, green
(3.0 V) 0.6 mA, blue (3.1 V) 0.3 mA. Actual V_F of R183455 **not verified**;
InGaN blue at 3.2 V would be 0.03 mA — barely on. Bench-visible in all cases;
through a translucent lid, blue is doubtful. If it matters, R13/R14 want 100 Ω
(not on the parts list — would have to be bought) — the GPIO's own ~30 Ω output
resistance then limits the current, still under the 12 mA drive setting.
Unchanged.

### 2.8 3V3 rail noise into U2 — **C5 added; JP4 makes it measurable**

The RT6154 buck-boost ripples at ~20 mV p-p, at ~1 MHz in PWM and in
irregular bursts in PFM at light load — the bursts fall in band. MCP6292 PSRR
at 100 kHz–1 MHz is of order 40 dB (typical curve, **not a datasheet limit**):
0.2 mV input-referred, ×121 = 24 mV at the ADC — 15 % of the wanted signal,
mostly not at 200 kHz but not nothing. VREF itself is clean (0.3 Hz filter).
Added **C5, 10 µF electrolytic** (the C4 part) beside C3 on the U2 rail; it
helps the PFM bursts, not the 1 MHz edge. The decisive experiment is **JP4 open,
AFE from a second cell or a bench supply at TP6**, noise floor compared — M7.
An RC in the feed (330 Ω, a part on hand) would drop 0.66 V at U2's 2 mA and
eat headroom: not recommended.

## 5. Confirm on a physical part before layout

Status after the owner checked the parts on 11 Sep 2026 is in bold.

1. **SS-12F23G5 geometry** — **closed, and it was wrong.** Photographs and
   measurements of the real part show the first footprint had the body
   standing 3.3 mm *behind* the pin row with the handle vertical. It is a
   right-angle switch: the body lies on the board **in front of** the pin row
   and the handle points sideways, parallel to the PCB. Measured: 6.3 mm from
   the pin row to the front face, 11.3 mm to the handle tip, 0.8 mm of body
   behind the pins, 5.5 mm tall. The drawing's "3.3" is the pin length, as the
   owner said, and is not used for anything. Footprint rewritten by
   `tools/gen_sw_footprint.py`; pads and ear holes are unchanged, so the
   netlist is unaffected. **Layout:** the board edge goes at y = +6.3 (marked
   on Dwgs.User) — body on the board, all 5 mm of handle outside it.
2. **SS-12F23G5 mounting-ear hole pitch, 12.1 mm, and hole size** — the
   footprint drills 1.15 mm at ±6.05 mm, and the photographs confirm the two
   ears are bent-down tabs in the same row as the three terminals. Confirm
   with calipers that they drop into 1.15 mm holes at that pitch.
3. **Tact switch SW2 leg pitch: 6.5 mm × 4.5 mm** and which pairs are joined
   — **confirmed by the owner:** the 6.5 mm pair is joined, the 4.5 mm pair
   is switched. Matches `SW_PUSH_6mm`. Closed.
4. **JST-XH header row pitch 2.50 mm** against the 2.54 mm LED legs for J3:
   trial-fit a 5 mm LED in a spare header.
5. **C4/C5 lead pitch 2.5 mm, body 5 mm** — one caliper reading.
6. **1N5819 body length** fits the 10.16 mm horizontal footprint with the
   leads bent — trial-fit; if the owner's parts are the longer DO-41 variant,
   use the 12.7 mm footprint.
7. **The Pico 2 W's RP2350 stepping** — **the owner's only Pico 2 W is
   RP2350A2: E9 is live.** §2.4 is therefore mandatory, not precautionary:
   input buffer off on GP2 when high-Z, TP2 measured at M3 with JP2 bridged,
   C6 fitted if it reads a rail. Check the marking on the second Pico 2 W
   when it arrives for M6.
8. **Whether the KP384455 pack has a protection PCB** — **confirmed, it does.**
   No fuse on the board. Closed.
9. **MCP6292 orientation mark on the actual MSOP-8** vs the footprint's pin-1
   chamfer — the one part on this board that cannot be desoldered twice.

## Not verified, said plainly

- MCP6292 PSRR at 100 kHz–1 MHz (§2.8): typical-curve reading, not a limit.
- The LED's forward voltages (§2.7).
- The 10 pF input capacitance the loss budget rests on (design §17 open item;
  M7 measures it). Every AC number in §2.2 scales with it.
- The body-hum amplitude assumed in §2.2 (1 V, 100 pF): an order-of-magnitude
  figure from bio-potential practice, not a measurement of this pad.
- The RP2350's ordinary (non-E9) leakage: datasheet limit ±1 µA, typical nA;
  the design only works at the typical figure, which is why M3 measures it.

The alternative re-homing for BTN/ROLE — GP22 (pin 29) and GP19 (pin 25) — was
checked and rejected. It clears the antenna keep-out, but the escapes then have
to detour around J2 and the corner boss to reach SW2 and JP8 at the hand end:
more copper near the antenna, not less. Pins 21 and 22 sit 1.3 mm and 1.6 mm
inside the keep-out's edge, and both nets are static DC — a pull-up input read
once at boot, and a strap to GND. Nothing switching or RF-carrying goes under
the antenna.

### J4 is no longer a connector

Ten separate through-hole breakout pads, **E1–E10**, each placed inboard of its
own Pico pin: 3V3 (36), RUN (30), GP0 (1), GP1 (2), GP4 (6), GP5 (7), GP20 (26),
GP21 (27), GP27 (32), GND (3). 2.0 mm pad on a 1.0 mm drill.

The nets are unchanged. Ten `TestPoint` symbols were chosen over a 1×10 symbol
with a scattered footprint for two reasons: a single 1×10 footprint cannot
describe ten positions on both sides of the board, and a connector symbol whose
pins are nowhere near each other lies to anyone reading the sheet. It also makes
the netlist oracle stricter — `EXPECTED_NETS` now names `("E7", "1")` rather
than `("J4", "7")`, so a mis-numbered header pin can no longer hide inside a net
that still has the right number of nodes. The 1×10 female comes off the parts
table; the 1×40 strip is now only the Pico's two 1×20s.

### Values settled, both of them the review's own recommendations

- **R3 10 MΩ → 1 MΩ** (§2.3). Recovery on a body falls from 3.6 ms to 0.36 ms —
  inside the §9.7 budget with margin, where it was missed by 3.6× — and the
  leakage a high-Z TX pin can inject before stage 1 clips rises from 15 nA to
  145 nA. Cost: 0.6 dB at 200 kHz, 2.4 dB at 40 kHz. Uses the R1/R2 part.
- **C1 100 nF → 330 pF C0G** (§2.2). The interstage high-pass moves from 15.9 Hz
  to 4.8 kHz: 40 dB at 50 Hz, and stage 2's hum limit rises 11× to 147 mV at the
  10 MΩ node, which makes stage 1 the binding constraint instead. The interstage
  time constant falls from 10 ms to 33 µs, so the "13 ms more" recovery term in
  §2.3 vanishes whatever firmware does. Cost: −0.06 dB at 40 kHz. Uses the C2
  part.

Both are value-only swaps on pads that did not move, so M7 can still A/B them
against the design-doc values. Keep the 10 MΩ (Robu 574992) for that comparison.

### Physical

- **D1 → the 7.62 mm horizontal DO-41 footprint.** The 10.16 mm one does not fit
  the 9.5 mm strip between J5 and the corner boss. The owner measured the actual
  1N5819 against 7.62 mm on 11 Sep 2026: it fits, so D1 stays on the
  little-finger strip and SW2 keeps its centred position at x = 20. §5.6 closed.
- **H1–H4 → `handoff:MountingHole_3.4mm_M3_Boss8mm`**, a new project footprint
  (`tools/gen_mount_footprint.py`), because KiCad 10 ships 3.2, 3.5 and 3.7 mm
  and no keep-out on any of them. 3.4 mm is the ISO 273 medium clearance: the
  board is screwed into a printed boss carrying a 5 mm brass insert, so the screw
  is never a locating feature and the extra 0.2 mm swallows the boss's position
  tolerance. The 8 mm keep-out rides in the footprint rather than being drawn on
  the board, so DRC enforces it wherever the hole goes and it cannot be forgotten
  on the fourth corner. Still unplated and untied — design §8.4, exactly one
  ground-plane connection.

### What it cost — three compromises, stated plainly

1. **Design §12.3 is no longer met.** "Op-amp far from the Pico and its antenna"
   is overturned: the AFE is now SMD on the top face in the 10 mm channel
   *directly under the Pico*. Once the ten breakout pads take their positions
   inboard of their own pins, that channel is the only contiguous area left. This
   is a decision, not an oversight, and it is measurable: open JP4, run the AFE
   from a second cell or a bench supply at TP6, compare noise floors — M7, the
   experiment §2.8 already specified. If the SMPS wins, the answer is a shield
   can or a different board, not a re-route.
2. **Stacking the pad under the cell restores roughly 16 pF of pad-to-return
   shunt** — the figure design §8.2 rejects for a two-sided board. The owner
   chose the stack so cell and pad share one four-sided pocket. The mitigation is
   2–3 mm of foam between cell and pad, which also stops the cell chafing. M7's
   loss measurement is the test; if it reads worse than the budget, the foam gets
   thicker before anything else changes.
3. **The cell sits partly under the antenna keep-out.** Nothing else fits there
   once the pad is centred. If BLE range disappoints, the fix is a smaller pad,
   not a smaller cell — pad area is what the link budget can most afford to lose.

### Housekeeping

`hardware/tools/floorplan.py` is deleted. It was the superseded first draft: a
54 × 65 board with the origin at the elbow/thumb corner and +y toward the hand,
against the settled 40 × 62 with +y toward the elbow. It wrote to the *same*
`hardware/floorplan.svg` path as `gen_floorplan.py`, so running it would have
silently replaced the real floor plan with a plan of a board that no longer
exists. (The brief recorded it and `floorplan.svg` as untracked; they were in
fact already committed in 194b4d3.)
